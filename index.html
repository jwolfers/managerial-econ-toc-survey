Build your table of contents

We are writing a new business economics textbook, and we want its structure to match the way you actually teach. Set the number of class sessions to your own course length, then drag the chapters on the right into those sessions, in the order you'd cover them — and toss anything you wouldn't teach into the unused pile. Click any title for details. Teach something we haven't listed? Add it to the session you'd teach it in.

Two things before you start. Chapter 1 isn't a preface — its cost-benefit toolkit is what every later chapter draws on, so it can only be taught first. We've put it in Session 1 to begin with, though it's yours to move. A handful of later chapters likewise build on earlier ones (each part's opening chapter, mostly), so the builder won't let one land ahead of a chapter it depends on — it will say which chapter is in the way. Everything else is free.

Survey editor

Three things to set: the chapters respondents sort, the questions they answer around the sort, and which round of surveying their answers are filed under.

Edit titles, parts, section lists, notes, and prerequisites. A prerequisite means that chapter must be scheduled before this one.

Every question respondents answer, in the order they see them. Each one sits either before the chapter sort or after it; drag a card by its handle (or use ▲▼) to reorder, and drag it across to move it to the other side.

Before the chapter sort

Shown at the top of the page, above the class sessions — screening or context questions respondents answer before they start building.

After the chapter sort

Shown under “A few final questions”, below the two built-in free-text prompts and above the name/email fields.

A round is simply a name for a stretch of surveying — Test while you’re trying things out, then the real thing, then perhaps 2027 when the book is revised. Every response is stamped with whichever round was running when it arrived, so the results dashboard can show one round at a time.

Starting a round changes nothing about the survey itself and deletes nothing: responses already collected keep the label they already have, and the survey link stays the same. Reusing a name switches back to that round rather than starting a second one.

Results dashboard

How instructors would structure the book, aggregated across every response. Chapter positions are normalized to each respondent's course length, so a 10-session and a 28-session course are comparable.
